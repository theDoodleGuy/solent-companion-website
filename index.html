Three questions your chart app doesn't answer

Today the answers are spread across four to six apps, harbour websites and VHF 12. Solent Companion puts them on one screen you can check in a few seconds, one-handed.

  • Is that ship about to need the water I'm in?

    In the Thorn Channel area, every ship over 150 m has a zone around it, 1,000 m ahead and 100 m either side, that small craft need to keep out of. It's called a Moving Prohibited Zone (MPZ). One coming up from astern is easy to miss.

  • What has the harbour put out today?

    Harbour authorities each publish their own notices. Regattas, dredging and works change where you can go, often for just a few days.

  • Will I float over Bramble Bank on this tide?

    Bramble Bank, Lepe Spit and Black Rock see groundings every season. The Day Skipper sum is easy at the chart table and harder in a seaway.

What it does

Built for these waters, and only these waters

Will you float over it?

Ten of the Solent's shallow spots, from Bramble Bank and Lepe Spit to Chichester Bar and Hurst Narrows. Put in your draft and the charted depth from your own chart, and the app shows the sum with the predicted height of tide. Tide figures are predictions, so it's a check on your own working, not a replacement for it.

  • Clearance now or at the time you choose, and when it's above your margin over the next 24 hours.
  • Every answer opens up to show the working, line by line.
  • Not sure which figure to use? The app shows you how to read the depth off your chart.
  • Tide curves for Solent ports, double high waters and stands included, from UK Hydrographic Office predictions.

Big ships, and the water they need

See the large ships as they broadcast their positions (AIS), turned to their heading. In the Thorn Channel area, each ship over 150 m gets its MPZ drawn ahead of it, with a dashed outline because the reported position is never exact. Positions can be minutes old, and not every vessel shows up.

  • Fast craft and ferries stand out, with a line showing where they'll be in a few minutes.
  • Tap a ship for its name, length, speed and how old its position is.
  • Old positions turn grey and say how many minutes old they are.

What's on the water today

Regatta, dredging, works: notices from the Solent's harbour authorities, in one list and as shaded areas on the map. Each one says when it applies and links to the authority's full notice.

  • Areas only show while they're in force, so the map doesn't fill with old ones.
  • Short plain-English summaries; the authority's own notice always applies.
  • In the closed test some notices are examples while the real feeds are added.

Phone in your pocket

Switch on Underway when you cast off. With the screen off, it can give you an advisory alert when:

  • your track crosses a big ship's MPZ, or you're already inside one;
  • a fast craft is closing on you;
  • a tide gate near you is below your margin, once you've added its charted depth from your chart.

Alerts can be late, missed or wrong, so keep your lookout and VHF 12. It stops by itself after 12 hours, and tells you plainly when it can't alert you, for example with no GPS fix or notifications turned off.

Easy on your night eyes

Red on black, so the screen doesn't spoil your night vision on the late tide. A higher-contrast option is there when you need to read the small print.

Your position stays on your phone

We never receive your position. No account, no ads, no tracking. Your boat details stay on your phone too. Read the privacy policy.

The limits: an aid, not a navigator

What it will and won't do. We'd rather you knew before you download it.

  • Ship positions can be minutes old. AIS can be delayed or incomplete, and not every vessel transmits.
  • Tide figures are predictions. Weather, pressure and surge change the actual height.
  • Depths come from your own chart. Clearance is only as good as the charted depth and draft you enter.
  • Alerts are advisory. They can be late, missed or wrong.

It doesn't replace your chart, your lookout or VHF 12. Keep a proper lookout, monitor VHF 12, and use your own judgement and charts at all times. Read the full disclaimer.

Questions

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet. We're starting with Android to get it right in one place first.

How much does it cost?

It's free, with no account and no ads.

Does it replace my chart app?

No. Solent Companion isn't a chart plotter and doesn't plan routes. It sits alongside your chart app, whether that's savvy navvy, Navionics, a plotter or paper charts, and adds the local layer they don't carry.

Will it work without signal?

Partly. The app keeps the latest tides and notices on your phone, and shows the last ship positions it had, with how old they are. Ships aren't live without a connection, and the app says so.

Where does the data come from?

Ship positions broadcast by the ships themselves (AIS), tide predictions from the UK Hydrographic Office, seamarks from OpenSeaMap, and notices from the Solent's harbour authorities. Tide gate clearance uses the charted depth you take from your own chart.

What does it do with my location?

It uses your position on your phone to show where you are and, in Underway mode, to work out alerts. We never receive it. The map providers do see which area of map you're viewing, as the privacy policy lists, along with everything else the app fetches.

Does it cover the Channel or other harbours?

Not at launch. It covers the Solent, from the Needles to Chichester Bar. Tell us where you'd like it next.
